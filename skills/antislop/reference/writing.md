# Writing

Read this before you write any text a person will read: page copy, buttons, error messages, a README, docs, a commit message, or a reply to the user.

AI text is recognised the same way AI design is. The words are the most likely next words, so they sound like everyone else's. The fix is the same too: say the specific thing that is true here.

These patterns also appear in human writing. One of them proves nothing. A page full of them reads as generated, and more importantly it reads as empty.

## How to write instead

1. **Know the point first.** Before writing a section, state its point in one sentence. If you cannot, you do not have one yet.
2. **Lead with it.** The first sentence carries the answer, the news, or the action. Background comes after, if at all.
3. **Use the specific noun and the real number.** "Exports 10,000 rows in about two seconds" instead of "blazing-fast exports". If you do not have the number, describe what happens instead of praising it.
4. **Use plain verbs.** "Is", "has", "does", "sends", "costs". A sentence that says what a thing does needs no adjective.
5. **Write to one reader.** Picture one person who needs this and talk to them. "You" is fine. So is "we" when a real team is speaking.
6. **Let length follow content.** Some sentences are short. Some need a clause or two to hold the thing together. Do not alternate on purpose.
7. **Stop when you are done.** No closing line that sums up, cheers, or offers more.
8. **Read it aloud.** Cut whatever you would not say across a table to someone you respect.

## Punctuation tells

| Tell | Example | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dashes around an aside or before a punchline | "Our tool — built for speed — just works." | Use commas, brackets or two sentences. Zero em dashes is a safe target for web copy. |
| A comma, then "and", then one more thing tacked on | "It saves time, cuts costs, and helps your team grow." | Say the one or two things that are true. Drop the third. |
| A comma, then an "-ing" phrase that claims significance | "We launched in 2019, marking a new chapter." | End the sentence at the fact. |
| A colon that sets up a reveal | "Here's the thing: it works." | Say the thing. |
| A question answered at once | "The result? Fewer bugs." | "It produced fewer bugs." |
| Semicolons or brackets swapped in for every dash you removed | | Rewrite the sentence. Do not trade one tic for another. |
| Exclamation marks for warmth | "Welcome back!" | Plain statement. |
| Ellipses for drama, arrows in prose | "And then... it clicked." | Cut. |

## Structure tells

- **Three of everything.** Three adjectives, three benefits, three bullet points, three short fragments ("Fast. Simple. Yours."). Count what is true and list that many.
- **"Not X, but Y."** Also "It's not just X, it's Y", "X, not Y", "less X, more Y", "more than just". These deny something nobody claimed. State Y.
- **"Whether you're X, Y, or Z."** Name the reader you built this for.
- **"From X to Y."** "From startups to enterprises." Say who actually uses it.
- **Every paragraph the same shape.** Same length, same opening word ("We help", "We believe", "We build"), same closing beat.
- **Signposting.** "Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know", "Now that we've covered X", "In conclusion", "In summary". Go straight to the content.
- **A summary after a short text.** If the reader just read it, do not repeat it.
- **Bullets that begin with a bold label and a colon,** each followed by one sentence, each the same length. Write a sentence or a table.
- **Headings that could sit on any site.** "Features", "Why choose us", "How it works", "Our mission", "Get in touch". Name what is in the section.
- **Title Case In Every Heading.** Sentence case.
- **An emoji in front of each heading or bullet.**
- **Bold scattered through a paragraph** to mark "key" phrases.

## Vocabulary

Cut on sight, then check the sentence still says something:

seamless, robust, powerful, elevate, empower, unlock, unleash, supercharge, streamline, leverage, utilize, harness, foster, enhance, optimize, revolutionize, transform, reimagine, delve, navigate, embark, craft (as a verb for writing or making software), curate, tailor, cutting-edge, next-generation, state-of-the-art, world-class, best-in-class, game-changing, innovative, holistic, comprehensive, effortless, intuitive, blazing-fast, lightning-fast, vibrant, rich, dynamic, pivotal, crucial, vital, key, testament, tapestry, landscape, realm, journey, ecosystem (when it is not one), solution (when it is a product), experience (when it is a page)

Phrases: "in today's fast-paced world", "in the ever-evolving landscape of", "at the heart of", "take it to the next level", "your trusted partner", "we understand that", "look no further", "say goodbye to", "the future of", "built for the modern", "designed with you in mind", "peace of mind", "it's worth noting that", "it's important to remember", "when it comes to", "at the end of the day", "a wide range of", "plays a crucial role", "stands as", "serves as", "boasts", "nestled in".

Do not swap a cut word for its nearest synonym. If "seamless" was hiding the lack of a fact, "smooth" hides it just as well.

## Tone tells

- **Praise for the reader or the question.** "Great question", "You're absolutely right", "What a fantastic idea".
- **Announcing what you will do** before doing it. "I'll walk you through", "Let me break this down".
- **Closing offers.** "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like", "Feel free to reach out".
- **Hedging on everything.** "can help", "may potentially", "in many cases", "generally speaking". Hedge once, where the doubt is real, and say what the doubt is.
- **Fake candour.** "Honestly", "to be fair", "let's be real", "the truth is", "no fluff", "no BS".
- **Reassurance nobody asked for.** "You're not alone", "and that's okay", "don't worry".
- **Vague authority.** "Experts agree", "studies show", "many users report", with no source.
- **Text about itself.** Lines that describe the page, explain the design, or point out how honest or minimal it is.

## Interface text

- **Buttons name the action and its object.** "Save changes", "Send invoice", "Delete 3 files". Not "Submit", "Continue", "Get started", "Learn more".
- **Errors say what happened and what to do.** "That email is already registered. Sign in instead, or use a different address." Not "Oops! Something went wrong."
- **Empty states say what will appear and how to add the first one.** No "Nothing to see here yet!" and no illustration of an empty box.
- **Labels are nouns the user already uses.** Not internal names, not marketing names.
- **Confirmation text repeats the consequence.** "Delete the project and its 14 files? This cannot be undone."
- **No greeting, no cheer, no personality in places people pass through fifty times a day.**
- **Keep the same word for the same thing** on every screen.

## READMEs and docs

- The first line says what the project is, in words a stranger understands.
- The second thing is how to run it.
- No emoji in headings. No row of badges that carry no information. No "Built with love". No "blazingly fast" without a benchmark.
- A feature list of bold labels with one-line blurbs is the README version of three identical cards. Show a command and its output instead.
- State what it does not do, and what is unfinished.
- Numbers come with how they were measured.

## Other languages

Write in the page's language from the first word. Do not write English and translate. Each of the patterns above has a local equivalent, and the borrowed English idiom ("take it to the next level" rendered word for word) is the first thing a native reader notices. Use the punctuation, quotation marks and number formats of that language.

## Check

Read every sentence and ask:

1. Does it tell the reader something they did not know?
2. Could a competitor print it unchanged?
3. Is there a number, name or example I could put in place of an adjective?
4. Did I write three things because there were three?
5. Would I say this out loud?
